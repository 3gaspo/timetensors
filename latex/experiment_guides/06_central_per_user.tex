\section{Theoretical overview and goal}
A central model estimates one mapping from all users and can transfer common
dynamics; a per-user model specializes but sees less data.  Window-weighted MSE
can favor users with more accessible windows, so the experiment also uses the
equal-user mean and the worst-user tail
\[
 \Wten(\ell)=\frac{1}{\lceil0.1U\rceil}
 \sum_{u\in\operatorname{Top}_{\lceil0.1U\rceil}}\bar\ell_u.
\]

\goal{Measure the benefit and fairness cost of cross-user learning, and compare
specialization with shared forecasting for trained and pretrained backbones.}

\section{Implementation details}
PatchTST is trained centrally or independently per user.  Chronos central
inference enables cross-learning across the batch; per-user inference disables
it.  All variants use identical temporal splits and evaluation queries.  The
combined table reports ordinary MSE, equal-user MSE, and \Wten.

This is a later-stage sweep after the data and optimization choices are fixed.
The study profile spans six datasets, three settings, three seeds, and both
PatchTST and Chronos. PatchTST uses the common $10^{-5}$ learning rate, batch
size 256, 10,000 optimizer steps, and 1,000-step validation. The test profile
runs one PatchTST Electricity case.

\textbf{Launcher:} \code{07\_central\_per\_user.slurm}

Per-user training creates multiple fitted models and must retain user identity
through evaluation.  Central and per-user results intentionally share one table
because the training scope is the independent variable.

\section{Interpretation}
A lower window-weighted MSE with worse \Wten{} indicates that cross-user learning
benefits the average while harming the tail.  Per-user gains may reflect
specialization but should be interpreted with their greater model count and
lower data per fit.  Chronos cross-learning is an inference-mode comparison,
whereas PatchTST is a training-scope comparison.
